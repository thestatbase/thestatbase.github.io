Project Overview and Goal

A receiver can receive a large share of his team's passing opportunities without producing the fantasy points that would typically come with that role. The goal of this project was to identify those differences, then determine if they could be used to predict a change in the player's future production.

I used linear regression to compare a receiver's opportunity with his fantasy points per game in 1 point-per-reception scoring. Players below the league regression line were given positive potential, while players above it were given negative potential. The theory was that players producing less than their opportunity would suggest may improve, while players producing considerably more may decline.

The first year produced different results depending on which part of the chart was evaluated. The receivers specifically marked with Most Potential improved in 10 of 12 evaluated windows. However, positive potential across the broader group of receivers was much less successful. Both findings are important when determining how the chart should be interpreted.

Numerical scoring error

The regression line was also evaluated as an estimate of future scoring. Its mean absolute error was 4.55 PPR points per appearance, compared with 4.68 when using the player's current average. Balanced direction accuracy, which gives increases and declines equal weight, was 56.2%. The estimated error improvement was small, and the player-bootstrap range included no improvement.

Methodology and Definitions

Opportunity and Production

WOPR, or Weighted Opportunity Rating, combines a receiver's target share and air-yards share. Target share measures how many of a team's pass attempts are directed toward the receiver. Air-yards share measures how much of the team's intended receiving distance belongs to him. Combining the two provides a way to evaluate the opportunity the receiver has received, rather than relying only on the yards or touchdowns he has produced.

WOPR = 1.5 × target share + 0.7 × air-yards share
Original chart x = 100 × WOPR × selected week / recorded player games
Chart y = full-PPR fantasy points / recorded player games

The original horizontal axis includes the selected-week and player-game adjustment shown above. WOPR itself is already based on shares, so this adjustment can change a player's position when he has missed games. The chart's x value should therefore be read as the original opportunity index, rather than an unadjusted WOPR share.

Potential and the Regression Line

A least-squares line describes the relationship between the opportunity index and current scoring among the plotted receivers. Potential is the fitted scoring value minus the player's current scoring average. It is the negative of the usual regression residual.

Potential = fitted PPR per game - current PPR per game

For example, a receiver averaging 10 points whose position on the line corresponds to 13 would have +3 potential. That describes a gap of 3 points between his current production and the fitted relationship. It does not establish that he will score exactly 13 points in the future.

How the Results Were Evaluated

Each eligible chart selection was compared with scoring in the next three scheduled games of the player's cutoff team. A window is one player evaluated from one chart, so the same receiver can appear in several windows. Improvement means the later average exceeded the average at publication; decline means it was lower. Ties do not count as a correct change.

Appearance-based averages use games with recorded player statistics. Team-game measures also include games without a later player appearance, counting those as zero. Windows without three remaining regular-season games, or without the required same-team follow-up, are excluded from the primary three-game test. One ambiguous Week 14 WR potential arrow was also excluded.

What the Boxes Showed

The green and yellow boxes were evaluated using the rectangles that appeared on each original chart. A player was included when the center of his dot fell inside the published rectangle. The boundaries were measured from the charts rather than replaced with new boundaries chosen after the results were known.

Notable Individual Predictions

A.J. Brown: Most Potential after Week 11

Brown averaged 11.30 PPR points per appearance when selected. His later three-game average was 25.40, an increase of 14.10 points. His three Most Potential selections had a large impact on the overall result: removing them reduces the mean improvement from +3.39 to +0.67 points. The remaining selections still improved in seven of nine windows.

Jordan Addison: Least Potential after Week 7

Addison's current average was 17.43 points, while his later average was 8.83. The decline of 8.60 points followed the direction identified by the chart. This is an example of negative potential correctly preceding a substantial decline.

Jerry Jeudy: Most Potential after Week 10

Jeudy was averaging 7.50 points when selected, but averaged 4.53 in the follow-up, a decline of 2.97. The selection was incorrect despite a positive potential score. It also shows why a result based on repeated selections must be distinguished from a result based on many different players.

Conclusions and Limitations

The first year supports a narrower conclusion than saying that every receiver with positive potential is likely to improve. The published Most Potential and Least Potential selections frequently moved in the indicated direction. However, the full signal was less successful, and its direction accuracy was below the always-decline baseline for receivers already averaging at least 10 points.

Please note that the 12 Most Potential selections involved only four distinct receivers. Their follow-up windows can overlap, and weekly repeat selections are not independent evidence. After removing identical player and endpoint windows caused by byes, the result was nine improvements in 11 windows. Counting only the first selection of each distinct receiver produced three improvements in four selections.

The chart also cannot fully account for injuries, changes in quarterback play, or changes in a player's role. The fitted line describes scoring and opportunity from the same period, so a large gap is not proof that the gap will disappear. Furthermore, a correctly predicted decline does not necessarily mean that the receiver stopped being productive: the Least Potential selections still averaged 13.42 later points per appearance.

Did each arrow identify the literal chart maximum or minimum?

This is a separate check of the label at publication, rather than a future scoring result. Among all displayed dots, Most Potential identified the residual leader on 9 of 13 unambiguous charts (69.2%); Least Potential identified the residual minimum on 4 of 14 (28.6%). Highest Production identified the scoring maximum on 11 of 14 (78.6%), while all 13 explicit Highest Volume labels identified the x-axis maximum.

A 0.01-point tolerance was used for rounding. The Week 15 Higher Volume caption was not graded as a Highest Volume claim. Different eligibility restrictions can change the literal leader, so the published selections above remain the selections actually marked on the charts.

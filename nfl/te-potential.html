Project Overview and Goal

A tight end can receive a large share of his team's passing opportunities without producing the fantasy points that would typically come with that role. The goal of this project was to identify those differences, then determine if they could be used to predict a change in the player's future production.

I used linear regression to compare a tight end's opportunity with his fantasy points per game in 1 point-per-reception scoring. Players below the league regression line were given positive potential, while players above it were given negative potential. The theory was that players producing less than their opportunity would suggest may improve, while players producing considerably more may decline.

Methodology and Definitions

Opportunity and Production

WOPR, or Weighted Opportunity Rating, combines a tight end's target share and air-yards share. Target share measures how many of a team's pass attempts are directed toward the tight end. Air-yards share measures how much of the team's intended receiving distance belongs to him. Combining the two provides a way to evaluate the opportunity the tight end has received, rather than relying only on the yards or touchdowns he has produced.

WOPR = 1.5 × target share + 0.7 × air-yards share
Chart X Axis = 100 × WOPR × selected week / recorded player games
Chart Y Axis = full-PPR fantasy points / recorded player games

Potential and the Regression Line

A least-squares regression line describes the relationship between the opportunity index and current scoring among the plotted tight ends. Potential is the fitted scoring value minus the player's current scoring average, also known as the negative of the player's residual.

Potential = fitted PPR per game - current PPR per game

For example, a tight end averaging 10 points whose position on the line corresponds to 13 would have +3 potential, meaning there is a gap of 3 points between his current production and his expected production based on the league-average regression line.

Conclusions and Limitations

It is important to note the following limitations with this project:

This model has not been forward tested yet for tight ends, though its wide-receiver counterpart shows promising potential for the concept to be successfully applied to tight ends as well. The chart cannot fully account for injuries, changes in quarterback play, or changes in a player's role. The fitted line describes scoring and opportunity from the same period, so a large gap is not proof that the gap will disappear.
